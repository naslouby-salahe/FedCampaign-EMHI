# Scientific Status Reconstruction

**Evidence cutoff: 2026-09-30.** This report reconstructs the current checkout, the project audit records, available experiment artifacts, and recent Git history. The repository contains pre-existing working-tree changes (`data/raw` is modified and `docs/audit/candidate-order.md` is untracked); neither was changed for this report. POC JSON/NPZ files under `docs/audit/pocs/` are frequently ignored local artifacts, so their existence and values are reported as local evidence, not as versioned release artifacts. ## 1. Current Project Idea

The project has changed identity and is not settled.

```text
original: FedCampaign-EMHI, global detection of constructed distributed attack campaigns
→ intermediate: test exclusion-matched higher-order interaction evidence against HOFD and operational baselines
→ current repository direction: find a defensible mechanism for collaborative IoT anomaly detection;
  the active leads are identity-conditioned dyadic residual collaboration on CIC IoT-DIAD
  and, as a support-limited fallback, benign-only peer transfer on Edge-IIoTset
```

The original question was whether coalition-level, complement-conditioned and proper-subset-purified evidence (Exclusion-Matched Hierarchical Innovation, EMHI) could warn on a constructed co-temporal distributed malicious episode before a fixed local detector policy. The intended paper/project identity is **FedCampaign-EMHI / Operational Distributed Insufficiency (ODI)**, with **Exclusion-Matched Irreducible Innovation (EMII)** as its theoretical principle. `README.md`, `docs/Roadmap.md`, and `docs/audit/Scientific Decisions.md` describe that study. Its main contribution is no longer supported by the primary result: Full EMHI and its order-at-most-two comparator tie, while order-three terms are unavailable on the real-data cohort and the local-warning endpoint is degenerate.

The newer untracked `docs/audit/candidate-order.md` (updated 2026-09-30) calls EMHI rejected as the main candidate and lists two active leads. The dyadic lead proposes modeling endpoint marginals and directed-pair residuals separately, then transferring cohort-supported pair summaries to detect a relationship that is unusual despite ordinary endpoint behavior. Its local CIC IoT-DIAD screen shows a dyad score above source/destination scores on the sampled captures, but capture, device, label, and identity confounding are not resolved. The Edge lead is benign-only peer support transfer; the release has only two eligible hosts and remains below the prior six-client rule. These are investigative leads, not a frozen study, algorithms, or accepted candidates. The ledger says explicitly that no candidate is accepted.

**Data actually available or inspected:** raw TON_IoT Network (23 CSVs; 22,339,021 rows), Edge-IIoTset (one CSV; 2,219,201 rows), and local CIC IoT-DIAD capture files used by the recent sampled dyad screen. N-BaIoT data were available for a small nine-device peer-shrinkage screen. These are not all equally suitable or currently selected: TON and Edge support the original pipeline; Edge fails the registered minimum-source rule; CIC's sampled capture split is not an admissible validation design yet; N-BaIoT's cheap screen did not establish a useful method. The controlled synthetic five-client benchmark is the selected mechanism benchmark in the detector-development work. Investigated but not established as usable include Gotham (license unclear), UNSW-IoTraffic (official archive inaccessible during check), and others listed in `docs/audit/Scientific Decisions.md`.

**Experimental units differ by phase.** The original real-data study uses temporal epochs and constructed campaign episodes over four retrospective TON source-IP groupings. Its ten confirmatory seeds rerandomize algorithm/training steps on the same trace, cohort, split, and registry; they are not independent networks. The synthetic detector work uses five clients (three generating the target interaction, two complement clients), coalition-coordinate/episode scores nested within 30 roots. The current CIC screen samples rows within capture files and is not a capture-held-out confirmatory experiment.

**Reusable infrastructure:** typed experiment registry and CLI, data preprocessing and complete-row identity, time splits/campaign registry, score/rank/context/projection/fusion/evaluation pipeline, checkpoint/resume and material-digest lifecycle, result export, synthetic generators, paired inference, and audit/POC harnesses. The original production EMHI implementation is present in `src/fedcampaign_emhi/`; experimental candidate implementations also exist in `docs/audit/pocs/`. Code presence establishes implementation only, not a selected or validated contribution.

**Strongest scientific signal:** controlled synthetic fitted-coordinate recovery is reproducible across the repaired 30-root study (mean recovery 0.9519, root-cluster-bootstrap 95% CI [0.8735, 1.0289]; proper-subset drift upper endpoint 0.0296). This demonstrates estimator feasibility for that controlled condition, not operational detection. The strongest real-data comparative result is negative: the corrected TON primary has paired ODI advantage 0, raw p=1. The newest dyad score screen is a preliminary positive lead (AUC 0.831 vs endpoint scores around 0.786–0.788), but its capture-label and identity confounding prevent a scientific claim.

## 2. Algorithm / Framework Status

```text
PARTIALLY
```

There is a specified and implemented EMHI framework, but the repository has **not** established a genuinely new, accepted algorithm/framework for the current scientific objective. EMHI's defined pieces are complement-only nuisance context, coalition interaction projection, proper-subset purification, bounded evidence factors, hierarchical aggregation, and an operational stop rule. The scientific audit found established neighboring mechanisms (functional ANOVA/HOFD, interaction tests, sequential testing, e-detectors, and federated anomaly detection), and the original comparative claim failed. The current primary does not exercise real order-three evidence, and its strict ODI successes are global stops at offset one with no finite local policies.

The current answer is therefore **PARTIALLY** only in the narrow sense that EMHI is a specified/implemented framework and controlled estimator components have passed feasibility checks. It is not honest to call EMHI a validated novel detector, to claim it improves ODI, or to rename the recent candidate list as an algorithm. The dyadic candidate still lacks a formally defined mechanism, leakage-safe protocol, valid unit of replication, baseline challenge, prior-art resolution, and full POC. The synthetic sparse-detector family has no selected candidate. No novel framework has cleared novelty, feasibility, and empirical-value gates together.

For historical EMHI, inputs are per-client temporal scores/ranks, coalition membership, and outside-coalition histories; outputs are coalition/order evidence and a global stopping decision. Its state comprises fitted contexts/projections, calibrated references and thresholds, and sequential evidence accumulators. The new computation beyond a simple score-fusion baseline is coalition-specific nuisance conditioning and purified interaction evidence with exclusion-matched information flow. This is a useful specification, but no exact prior-art match has been demonstrated and no broad novelty search was done. Feasibility evidence exists for synthetic projection; evidence of operational advantage does not.

## 10. Current Scientific Position

### What we know

- The corrected TON comparison completed 200/200 valid cells and found no Full-vs-order≤2 ODI advantage (paired difference 0, p=1).
- The endpoint is degenerate for the intended before-local claim on this trace: global stops occur at offset one and no finite local policy is available; strict ODI's recorded successes do not show an earlier-than-local warning.
- The four-client TON release cannot supply the required order-three complement support. Edge has two eligible hosts and remains `Not Tested` under the six-source rule.
- Synthetic estimator recovery and proper-subset integrity pass their stated gates in a repaired 30-root study, but candidate detector power/null-control gates did not. The newest matched-horizon 30-root work is unfinished, and the four-root provisional result fails null-control bounds.
- The 60-epoch pure-triple task is information-limited at theta=.1 and alpha=.05; radial norm evidence is reflection-blind to the signed target direction.
- CIC dyad scores improve the small sampled screen over endpoint scores, but the setup cannot distinguish relational signal from capture/device/label shortcuts.

### What we suspect

- Endpoint-conditioned dyadic residual sharing may provide a useful IoT collaboration mechanism if it survives capture-disjoint evaluation and endpoint-only controls.
- The controlled five-client benchmark may support a sparse-coordinate operational detector after the already specified development matrix completes; the provisional powers alone do not establish that outcome.
- A conservative contribution may be a careful mechanism/benchmark study separating coordinate recovery from operational detection and exposing when higher-order evidence is unavailable. That is not yet a new validated algorithm contribution.

### What we do not know

- Whether any current lead is novel relative to close endpoint-conditioned relational anomaly detection, peer transfer, and federated graph/pair methods.
- Whether a dyad effect survives address removal, identity changes, capture-held-out evaluation, and direct endpoint-only baselines.
- Whether the current 30-root matched-horizon detector matrix will pass all frozen gates; only part is complete.
- Whether any valid real dataset can support independent clients, aligned capture coverage, temporal splits, and enough benign support.
- Whether EMHI would have operational utility under data with enough clients, genuine capture coverage, finite local policies, and order-three support.

### What is currently defensible as a paper contribution

At present, only a conservative empirical/methodological account of the implemented EMHI specification, its controlled synthetic estimator-feasibility results, and the corrected negative TON comparison can be supported, with the fixed-trace, zero-event, no-order-three, and no-local-policy limits stated prominently. The newest CIC result may be described only as a screening observation motivating follow-up, not as validated relational detection. A new algorithm contribution is not currently established.

### What is not currently defensible

- That EMHI improves strict ODI, warns before local detectors, detects real order-three distributed campaigns, generalizes to Edge or multiple sites, or provides privacy.
- That the current sparse detector or dyadic candidate is novel, selected, or validated.
- That synthetic seed counts are independent real-world replications, disjoint windows are independent, or dense zero-filled TON periods prove continuous capture.
- Any “first” claim, population-level real-data false-alarm guarantee, or anytime-valid e-process claim.

### Most important unresolved bottleneck

The project lacks a **valid, identifiable empirical mechanism test** that both separates the proposed collaborative signal from simple endpoint/local baselines and has independent, leakage-safe evaluation units. In the original study, support and local-policy degeneracy prevent the real trace from testing the claimed higher-order ODI mechanism. In the new lead, capture/device/identity confounding prevents attributing the dyad screen's improvement to relational collaboration. Until one candidate passes that test, literature novelty by itself cannot support a journal-grade algorithm/framework contribution.
